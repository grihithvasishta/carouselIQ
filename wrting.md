# writing.md — The Da Vinci Writing Principles

Da Vinci didn't just observe things. He found the structure underneath the surface.
That's what elite carousel writing does: it makes the invisible visible, precisely.

These are not style tips. They are compression and precision protocols.
Apply them line by line.

---

## PRINCIPLE 1: SPECIFICITY IS CREDIBILITY

Vague language signals that you haven't gone deep enough.
Specific language signals that you've actually been there.

Every time you write a vague line, replace it with the most specific version that's still true.

| ❌ Vague | ✅ Specific |
|---------|-----------|
| "Many companies struggle with this" | "73% of Series A companies cite this as their top ops failure in year 2" |
| "This tool is really powerful" | "This cuts your integration from 14 steps to 3" |
| "Successful founders do this differently" | "Tobi Lütke still reviews Shopify's API changelog weekly" |
| "AI is changing the industry" | "Klarna replaced 700 customer service agents with one model. Average handle time dropped from 11 minutes to 2." |
| "Consistency matters" | "Post the same day, same time, for 90 days before drawing any conclusions about what works" |

**The specificity test:** Could this line appear in a carousel on any topic?
If yes → it's not specific enough. Drill deeper.

---

## PRINCIPLE 2: EVERY LINE EARNS ITS PLACE

Read each line and ask: if I delete this, does the slide lose meaning?
If no → delete it.

**Common lines that never earn their place:**
- "Now let's talk about..."
- "This is really important..."
- "Here's the thing:"
- "As you can see..."
- "In today's world..."
- "It's no secret that..."
- "At the end of the day..."

These are verbal filler. They signal that the real content hasn't arrived yet.
Cut them. Start with the real content.

---

## PRINCIPLE 3: THE COMPRESSION PROTOCOL

After writing a slide, compress it in three passes:

**Pass 1 — Cut the setup:**
The first sentence of most slides is setup. It tells the reader what you're about to say instead of saying it.
Delete any sentence that begins with "In this slide" or "Now we're going to discuss" or any version of them.

**Pass 2 — Collapse the adjectives:**
Find every adjective. Ask: does this adjective carry information, or does it just intensify?
- "incredibly important" → "important" or better: what makes it important specifically
- "extremely complex" → tell us what makes it complex
- "deeply insightful" → never. if it's insightful, the insight itself should make that obvious

**Pass 3 — The 30% Test:**
Read your slide. Could you say the same thing in 30% fewer words without losing meaning?
If yes → cut 30%. Then run the test again.

## PRINCIPLE 4: SHORT SENTENCES FOR IMPACT, LONGER FOR CONTEXT

Rhythm is not decoration. Rhythm controls what the reader remembers.

A short sentence after a longer one lands like a punch.

> "Most founders spend their first year optimizing things that won't matter if they don't solve the core distribution problem. They build the perfect product for a market they haven't found yet. That's the trap."

"That's the trap." — three words. Hits harder than any explanation could.

**The rule:**
- Context, background, nuance → longer sentences (12–20 words)
- Conclusions, insights, revelations → short sentences (3–8 words)
- Never put two short punchy sentences in a row — the second one loses all impact

---

## PRINCIPLE 5: CONCRETE NOUNS OVER ABSTRACT ONES

Abstract nouns make readers work. Concrete nouns do the work for them.

| ❌ Abstract | ✅ Concrete |
|-----------|----------|
| "success" | "closed the round / shipped the feature / hit 10k subscribers" |
| "growth" | "from 0 to 50k users in 8 months" |
| "leadership" | "the decision Bezos made when AWS was 3% of Amazon's revenue" |
| "failure" | "the pull request that took Cloudflare down for 6 hours" |
| "innovation" | "the feature 5 teams passed on that became Slack's most-used surface" |

Abstract nouns are placeholders. Replace them with the thing they're standing in for.

---

## PRINCIPLE 6: THE ACTIVE VERB RULE

Passive voice creates distance. Active voice creates presence.

| ❌ Passive | ✅ Active |
|-----------|---------|
| "Mistakes were made" | "They shipped without testing" |
| "It was decided that..." | "The CEO overruled the team" |
| "Users are often confused by..." | "The onboarding flow loses 60% of users on step 3" |
| "Growth can be achieved through..." | "You grow by doing one thing better than anyone else until people notice" |

**The passive voice test:** Find every "was," "were," "is," "are" + past participle.
Ask: who is doing the action? Name them. Make them do it.

---

## PRINCIPLE 7: SHOW THE MECHANISM, NOT THE CONCLUSION

Most carousels tell people what is true.
Elite carousels show people why it's true — and let them conclude it themselves.

The moment a reader concludes something themselves, they own it.
Owned ideas get shared. Told ideas get forgotten.

**Instead of:** "Compound interest is the most powerful force in finance."

**Show the mechanism:**
> "£1,000 at 8% return.
> Year 10: £2,158
> Year 20: £4,661
> Year 40: £21,724
> The last 20 years generates more than the first 20 combined.
> The growth isn't in the investment. It's in the waiting."

Let them feel the conclusion. Don't announce it.

---

## PRINCIPLE 8: NAME THE SPECIFIC ENEMY

Vague problems produce vague advice.
Named, specific problems produce insight that feels personal.

**Vague enemy:** "Many businesses struggle with retention"
**Named enemy:** "The free trial cliff — the 72-hour window where 60% of trial users decide whether to ever log in again"

Naming the enemy does three things:
1. It proves you understand the problem at a practitioner level
2. It gives the reader something to agree with ("yes, exactly that")
3. It makes the solution feel designed for their specific situation, not generic advice

---

## PRINCIPLE 9: THE ONE-THING RULE

Each slide communicates exactly one idea.

Not one topic. One idea.
"Social media strategy" is a topic.
"The reason most social strategies fail is that they optimize for reach before they've found the message" is one idea.

When a slide tries to carry two ideas:
- The reader has to choose which one to hold onto
- They usually hold onto neither
- The slide wastes its position on the ladder

**The test:** Summarize the slide in one sentence starting with "This slide argues that..."
If you need "and" → you have two slides. Split them.

---

## PRINCIPLE 10: END LINES WITH WEIGHT

In English, the last word of a sentence gets the most emphasis.
Use this deliberately.

| ❌ Weight at wrong end | ✅ Weight at right end |
|----------------------|----------------------|
| "This is a problem that compounds over time" | "This problem compounds" |
| "Most people never learn this until it's too late for them" | "Most people learn this too late" |
| "The algorithm rewards this kind of behaviour specifically" | "The algorithm rewards exactly this" |

Read your last word. Is it the word you want ringing in the reader's mind as they process the line?
If not → restructure the sentence until it is.
