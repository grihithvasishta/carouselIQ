# anti-patterns.md — The 12 Carousel Sins

These are the patterns that make carousels feel complete while delivering nothing.
Each one comes with a full before/after rewrite.

---

## SIN 1: THE WIKIPEDIA HOOK

**What it looks like:**
> "Emotional intelligence (EQ) is the ability to understand, use, and manage your own emotions in positive ways..."

**Why it fails:**
Definitions are for textbooks. Carousels are for shifts.
The reader already knows what EQ is. You just used slide 1 to tell them something they knew before they opened the carousel.

**Rewrite:**
> "High IQ predicts job performance. High EQ predicts *whose* career you don't accidentally derail on the way there. Most people optimize for the wrong one."

---

## SIN 2: THE LISTICLE IN DISGUISE

**What it looks like:**
> Slide 3: "Tip #1: Post consistently"
> Slide 4: "Tip #2: Engage with your audience"
> Slide 5: "Tip #3: Use strong visuals"

**Why it fails:**
There is no thread. No tension. No curiosity ladder.
The reader could read slides 3, 5, 7 in any order and lose nothing.
That means the carousel has no architecture — just content stacked in a box.

**Rewrite:**
Give the tips a narrative spine. Each one should be impossible to understand without the previous one.
> Slide 3: "The reason consistency fails for most people: they're being consistent about the wrong thing."
> Slide 4: "Consistent *format* without consistent *insight* is just noise with a schedule."
> Slide 5: "Here's what consistent insight actually looks like at the slide level."

---

## SIN 3: THE HYPE SLIDE

**What it looks like:**
> "AI is revolutionizing every industry. The companies that adapt will thrive. The ones that don't will be left behind."

**Why it fails:**
Zero information. Zero specificity. Zero tension.
This sentence could have been written in 2018 about cloud computing, in 2015 about mobile, in 2010 about social media.
It says nothing because it's true of every technological transition in history.

**Rewrite:**
> "In March 2024, Klarna announced one AI model was doing the work of 700 customer service agents. Average handle time dropped from 11 minutes to 2. Customer satisfaction scores went up.
> The job title didn't get automated. The performance floor did."

---

## SIN 4: THE MIRROR CTA

**What it looks like:**
> "So remember: consistency, clarity, and connection are the keys to growing your personal brand. Follow for more tips like these!"

**Why it fails:**
The last slide restated the hook with a follow button attached.
The reader learned nothing new on the final slide. The carousel ended exactly where it started.
This is the written equivalent of a movie ending with the trailer.

**Rewrite:**
> "The creators who scaled past 100k stopped thinking about 'what should I post?' and started asking 'what do I know that most people in this space have gotten wrong?'
> That question is the content strategy. Everything else is execution."

---

## SIN 5: THE ORPHAN INSIGHT

**What it looks like:**
Slides 1–4: generic setup
Slide 5: one genuinely non-obvious insight
Slides 6–8: generic conclusion

**Why it fails:**
The insight lands and then the carousel abandons it.
The reader gets the shift, then watches the carousel walk it back into platitudes.
One real insight surrounded by filler doesn't make an elite carousel — it makes an elite slide in a mediocre carousel.

**Fix:**
Every slide before the insight should build toward it.
Every slide after should be a consequence of it.
The insight is the spine. Everything else is tissue.

---

## SIN 6: THE HEDGED CLAIM

**What it looks like:**
> "Some research suggests that, in certain contexts, it might be possible that frequent posting could potentially have a positive impact on reach for some users."

**Why it fails:**
Hedging is not nuance. Nuance has a point.
Hedging signals that you don't trust your own claim enough to state it.
If you don't trust it, the reader won't either.

**Rewrite:**
State the claim cleanly. Then add the condition separately.
> "Posting frequency increases reach — up to a threshold. Past 2x per day on most platforms, incremental posts cannibalize each other's distribution."

Claim first. Condition second. Never blended into mush.

---

## SIN 7: THE INSPIRATIONAL CLOSER

**What it looks like:**
> "You have what it takes. Believe in yourself. Start today."

**Why it fails:**
Motivation without mechanism is just noise.
The reader feels something for 4 seconds and then it's gone, replaced by the next post.
Elite closers don't make you feel good. They give you one concrete thing to do or think differently.

**Rewrite:**
> "Pick the one carousel you've been "almost ready" to post for the last 2 weeks.
> The reason you haven't posted it is the reason it's probably the one worth posting."

---

## SIN 8: THE PASSIVE OBSERVATION

**What it looks like:**
> "It's interesting to note that many successful entrepreneurs share certain habits in common that seem to contribute to their outcomes."

**Why it fails:**
Passive voice, vague subjects, non-committal verbs.
This sentence contains no information. It is pure grammatical filler dressed up as observation.

**Rewrite:**
> "Bezos, Musk, and Gates all kept their calendars blocked for deep work before 10am. Not because they read a productivity book. Because they discovered independently that their best thinking happened before the first meeting."

Specific people. Specific behavior. Specific time. No passive voice.

---

## SIN 9: THE FALSE BINARY

**What it looks like:**
> "You can either optimize for likes or optimize for real connection. The choice is yours."

**Why it fails:**
Most real trade-offs aren't binary — they're spectrums with different optimal points depending on where you are.
False binaries feel like insight but they're actually just forced drama.
The reader who knows the space immediately spots the simplification and dismisses everything else you've said.

**Rewrite:**
> "Optimizing for engagement and optimizing for trust pull in different directions in the short term.
> The accounts that grow fastest in year 1 often plateau in year 3.
> The accounts that grow slowest in year 1 are often the ones with compounding leverage in year 5.
> The question isn't which to optimize for. It's which game you're playing."

---

## SIN 10: THE UNEARNED AUTHORITY

**What it looks like:**
> "As an expert in [field], I can tell you that..."
> "After years of experience, I've learned..."
> "Trust me on this one."

**Why it fails:**
Claiming authority is the weakest possible form of credibility.
Real authority shows up in the specificity of what you know — not in announcing that you know things.
The reader doesn't trust the claim. They trust the evidence.

**Rewrite:**
Never claim expertise. Demonstrate it.
Don't say "I've seen this hundreds of times."
Say the thing you only know from having seen it hundreds of times.

---

## SIN 11: THE PREMATURE CONCLUSION

**What it looks like:**
Slide 3 gives the answer.
Slides 4–8 just elaborate on the answer already given.

**Why it fails:**
The curiosity ladder collapses the moment the answer arrives.
Everything after slide 3 is reading the last chapter and then reading the rest of the book.
The reader is gone.

**Fix:**
The answer is always the last thing delivered.
Every slide before it makes the answer feel earned.
Every slide after it is a consequence that reframes the answer.
There is no such thing as "elaborating" — only deepening, evidencing, or reframing.

---

## SIN 12: THE TOPIC CAROUSEL

**What it looks like:**
> "Here are 7 things you should know about [broad topic]"
> Slide 2: Thing 1
> Slide 3: Thing 2
> [...]

**Why it fails:**
This is a Wikipedia disambiguation page formatted as slides.
There is no angle. No tension. No specific reader. No perspective shift.
It covers a topic without saying anything specific about it.

**Rewrite:**
Pick ONE thing from that list of 7.
Find the angle that makes it non-obvious.
Build the entire carousel around that one thing done at maximum depth.

Seven shallow things remembered for zero seconds.
One deep thing remembered for a year.
